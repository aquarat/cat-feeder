# General review — 2026-10-25

**Status: independent desk review by five read-only reviewers** (rev A circuit,
rev B circuit, JLCPCB DFM/DFA, system integration, mechanical/thermal). Items
marked **[re-checked]** were independently confirmed by the coordinating
session. All other items are reviewer findings or estimates. No physical part was
built or tested.

Related: [decisions D32–D35](decisions.md), [plenum thermal study](plenum-thermal.md),
[JLCPCB rule files](../electronics/dfm/README.md), [rev A](pcb-design.md),
[rev B](../electronics/revb/README.md), [direct-contact model](direct-contact-thermal.md).

## Verdict

**Do not quote either PCB.** Rev A has assembly and fit blockers. Rev B is
incomplete and its power path needs redesign. More fundamentally, the thermal and
mechanical concept findings change what the controller must do. The owner has since chosen
a 24 V brick (D32), all electronics in the case (D33), a hand-soldered
through-hole variant (D34) and is exploring a cold-air plenum (D35).

## JLCPCB rules

The official pages saved in `electronics/revb/dfm/evidence/` (re-fetch with
`electronics/revb/dfm/retrieve_sources.py`) match the live
pages on 2026-10-25, but they were stored as text, not encoded as checks.
KiCad rule files now live in [electronics/dfm](../electronics/dfm/README.md).
Still manual: 2.5 mm part-to-edge, fiducials and tooling holes, 4-layer inner
hole-to-copper 0.3 mm, and CPL rotation against JLC library footprints.

## Recommended sequence

1. Friction pull test: sector on deck quarter, dry, wet, cold and with dried gravy.
2. One wedge on a CPU cooler at +2 °C under a lid section. Compare direct
   contact with the D35 plenum on the same rig.
3. The same rig with the real sink and fan; compare the CP60440 with a lower-leak TARK-class module.
4. Bench rig: devkit, StepStick, sensors, fan, DPS5005 and one TEC. Measure DPS
   inrush and power-on state, input current and Wi-Fi inside the shell.
5. Then design the D34 through-hole board against the measured requirements.
